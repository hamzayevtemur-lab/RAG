Now let me provide more detail on our fourth quarter results by market platform.

DATA CENTER:
Q4 Data Center revenue of $18,404 million grew 27% sequentially and 409% year-over-year.
This was our fifth consecutive quarter of sequential Data Center revenue growth. Revenue was
driven by strong demand for our HGX H100 platform for both training and inference of large
language models, generative AI applications, and recommender systems.

Cloud service providers represented slightly more than half of our Data Center revenue.
Large consumer internet companies represented approximately 25% of Data Center revenue, with
the remainder from enterprise and sovereign AI customers.

Networking revenue within Data Center was $3,208 million in Q4, bringing full-year networking
revenue to $13,016 million, an increase of 133% year-over-year. InfiniBand revenue roughly
doubled year-over-year, driven by large-scale AI cluster deployments requiring end-to-end
InfiniBand networking for maximum GPU utilization.

Regarding China: Following the October 2023 export control rules, Q4 Data Center revenue
from China declined to approximately 9% of Data Center revenue, down from approximately 22%
in the first three quarters of the fiscal year. We are sampling our new export-compliant
products, including the H20, to customers in China. However, we expect competition from
domestic Chinese semiconductor companies (such as Huawei Ascend processors) to intensify,
and we cannot predict whether new regulations will further restrict our ability to sell
compliant products.

GAMING:
Q4 Gaming revenue was $2,865 million, up 56% year-over-year and up 9% sequentially, driven
by the continued ramp of GeForce RTX 40 Series GPUs for desktops and laptops based on the
Ada Lovelace architecture. DLSS 3 adoption continues to expand, with over 500 games and
applications now supporting DLSS technology.

PROFESSIONAL VISUALIZATION:
Q4 ProViz revenue was $463 million, up 105% year-over-year, driven by a broad recovery in
enterprise workstation spending and demand for RTX Ada Generation GPUs. Our Omniverse platform
continues to see adoption among large industrial manufacturers for digital twin workflows.

AUTOMOTIVE:
Q4 Automotive revenue was $281 million, up 4% year-over-year. DRIVE Orin design wins
continue to expand, with over 25 electric vehicle manufacturers now deploying DRIVE platforms
in production vehicles. We announced that our next-generation DRIVE Thor centralized compute
platform has been selected by BYD, the world's largest electric vehicle manufacturer, for
their next-generation intelligent driving architecture.

I will now turn the call over to Jensen.

JENSEN HUANG — FOUNDER, PRESIDENT & CEO:
Thank you, Colette. We had a spectacular quarter and fiscal year. NVIDIA's data center has
become the essential engine of the world's AI infrastructure.

Let me describe the major industry dynamics driving our growth:

1. THE TRANSITION TO ACCELERATED COMPUTING:
   General-purpose computing has run out of gas. CPU performance scaling has slowed from the
   historical 50% annual improvement to approximately 10% per year. Simultaneously, the
   computational demands of AI workloads are growing at 10x per year. Accelerated computing
   is the only viable path forward.

   To illustrate: training GPT-4-class models requires approximately 25,000 A100 GPU-years
   of compute. On CPUs, this same training would require over 1 million CPU server-years and
   consume 10x more energy. Accelerated computing is not just faster — it is fundamentally
   more energy efficient. An NVIDIA GPU system performing AI training is approximately 20x
   more energy efficient than a CPU-based system for the same task.

2. GENERATIVE AI — THE NEW APPLICATION PLATFORM:
   Generative AI is creating an entirely new category of software applications. Foundation
   models trained on broad data are being fine-tuned and customized for every industry. We
   are seeing sovereign nations invest in building their own AI infrastructure and training
   their own language models on national data, in their own languages.

   In Q4, we saw significant new cloud instance announcements from all major CSPs:
   - Microsoft Azure announced general availability of ND H100 v5 instances.
   - Amazon Web Services launched P5 instances powered by H100 GPUs with UltraCluster
     support for up to 20,000 H100 GPUs.
   - Google Cloud expanded its A3 Mega instances with H100 GPUs to additional regions.
   - Oracle Cloud Infrastructure deployed the world's largest H100 SuperPOD with 16,384 GPUs.

3. SOVEREIGN AI:
   Nations around the world are recognizing that AI infrastructure is critical national
   infrastructure. We are working with governments and national carriers in France, India,
   Japan, Singapore, the UAE, Italy, and others to build sovereign AI clouds.

4. NVIDIA INFERENCE PLATFORM:
   It's important to understand that NVIDIA GPUs are not just for training. Inference is now
   our fastest-growing data center workload. In Q4, inference represented approximately 40%
   of Data Center revenue, up from approximately 30% one year ago. Our inference advantage
   comes from the combination of Tensor Cores, TensorRT-LLM software, and the ability to
   run the largest models with the lowest latency and highest throughput.

QUESTION AND ANSWER SESSION
================================================================================

ANALYST: TIMOTHY ARCURI (UBS)
Q: Jensen, you gave Q1 guidance of $24 billion, which implies Data Center revenue above $19
billion. Can you help us understand the demand trajectory? Are you still supply-constrained,
and when do you expect supply to catch up with demand?

JENSEN HUANG:
Tim, demand remains substantially above supply. We are working extremely closely with TSMC
and our memory suppliers to increase supply of both Hopper and our upcoming Blackwell
products. We have secured multi-year capacity commitments for CoWoS advanced packaging and
HBM3e memory.

The constraint today is not semiconductor wafer supply — it is advanced packaging (CoWoS)
capacity and HBM3e memory availability. Both TSMC and SK Hynix are aggressively expanding
capacity throughout calendar year 2024, and we expect supply conditions to improve
progressively. However, I want to be clear: we expect demand to continue to significantly
exceed supply through at least the first half of fiscal year 2026.

ANALYST: STACY RASGON (BERNSTEIN)
Q: Colette, your Q4 gross margin was 76%. How should we think about gross margin trajectory
as you ramp Blackwell, which involves more complex packaging and liquid cooling?

COLETTE KRESS:
Stacy, as we ramp Blackwell in the second half of fiscal year 2025, we expect gross margins
to temporarily moderate from the Q4 high of 76% to the low-to-mid 70% range. New product
introductions typically carry higher initial costs due to lower yields, qualification costs,
and the ramp of new packaging technologies. As Blackwell matures and volumes scale, we
expect gross margins to recover toward the mid-70% range.

ANALYST: VIVEK ARYA (BANK OF AMERICA)
Q: Jensen, regarding China — with Data Center China revenue declining to 9% in Q4, do you
expect this to stabilize, increase with the H20, or continue to decline?

JENSEN HUANG:
Vivek, this is a dynamic situation. The H20 product is designed to be compliant with current
USG regulations, and we are seeing customer interest. However, I want to be transparent:
Chinese customers are simultaneously evaluating domestic alternatives, including Huawei's
Ascend 910B processor. We face competitive risks in China that we did not face two years ago.

Furthermore, the regulatory landscape continues to evolve. We cannot predict whether the USG
will further tighten performance thresholds or impose additional restrictions. We are planning
our business assuming that China will be a smaller portion of our revenue mix going forward
compared to historical levels.

ANALYST: AARON RAKERS (WELLS FARGO)
Q: Can you give us more detail on the Blackwell ramp timeline? When should we expect revenue
contribution?

COLETTE KRESS:
Aaron, we expect to begin shipping Blackwell products in the second half of fiscal year 2025,
which corresponds to the July-January 2025 timeframe. Initial shipments will be qualification
and early access units. We expect Blackwell to ramp to material revenue contribution in Q3
and Q4 of fiscal year 2025. We anticipate Blackwell to exceed Hopper revenue within a few
quarters of its ramp, given the significant performance improvements and strong customer demand.

ANALYST: MATT RAMSAY (TD COWEN)
Q: Jensen, you mentioned inference is now 40% of Data Center revenue. What's driving that
mix shift, and how does Blackwell's inference performance change the economics for your customers?

JENSEN HUANG:
Matt, this is one of the most important dynamics in our business. As enterprises move from
AI experimentation to production deployment, inference becomes the dominant workload.
A company might train a model once over weeks, but then serve inference requests billions
of times per day.

Blackwell's second-generation Transformer Engine with FP4 inference delivers up to 5x more
inference tokens per second per dollar compared to Hopper H100. This is transformative
because it dramatically reduces the total cost of ownership for running AI applications at
scale. We believe this will accelerate enterprise adoption because it makes the ROI calculation
for deploying generative AI applications significantly more compelling.

ANALYST: TOSHIYA HARI (GOLDMAN SACHS)
Q: Colette, can you quantify the mix between HGX H100 systems and the newer H200 in your
Q4 results, and what you expect for Q1?

COLETTE KRESS:
Toshiya, Q4 was predominantly H100 revenue. The H200 is just beginning to ramp, with initial
shipments to select cloud partners in Q4. We expect H200 to contribute more meaningfully to
revenue beginning in Q1 FY2025, particularly as hyperscale customers refresh and expand their
clusters for inference-heavy workloads where the larger 141GB HBM3e memory capacity provides
a significant advantage.

ANALYST: HARLAN SUR (JP MORGAN)
Q: Jensen, can you discuss the enterprise AI opportunity beyond hyperscalers? What industries
are you seeing the fastest adoption?

JENSEN HUANG:
Harlan, enterprise AI is our next major growth vector. We're seeing strong early adoption in:

1. Financial Services: Major banks and hedge funds are deploying NVIDIA DGX systems for
   proprietary trading models, risk analytics, and regulatory compliance automation.

2. Healthcare and Life Sciences: Drug discovery, medical imaging (digital pathology,
   radiology AI), and genomic analysis. The NVIDIA Clara platform has been adopted by
   over 50 hospitals and research institutions.

3. Telecommunications: Network optimization, customer service automation, and 5G RAN
   intelligent controllers. Major carriers including T-Mobile and Deutsche Telekom are
   deploying NVIDIA AI-on-5G platforms.

4. Manufacturing: Predictive maintenance, quality inspection (computer vision on production
   lines), and supply chain optimization. BMW, Foxconn, and Siemens are building digital
   twins with NVIDIA Omniverse.

5. Automotive: Beyond ADAS — fleet management, autonomous trucking, and in-vehicle AI.

We expect enterprise to grow from roughly 25% of Data Center revenue today to a significantly
larger portion over the next 2-3 years.
