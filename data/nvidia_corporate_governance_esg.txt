2.3 Compensation Philosophy:
NVIDIA's compensation program is designed to:
- Attract and retain world-class engineering and leadership talent in an intensely competitive
  market (competing with Apple, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and leading AI startups for talent).
- Align executive compensation with long-term shareholder value creation through equity-heavy
  pay structures (approximately 85% of CEO total compensation was equity-based in FY2024).
- Tie annual cash bonuses to measurable financial performance metrics including revenue
  growth, operating margin, and strategic milestones.

after a one-year cliff. The Compensation Committee uses a peer group of 15 technology
companies for benchmarking, including AMD, Broadcom, Intel, Qualcomm, Apple, Alphabet,
Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Salesforce, Adobe, Synopsys, Cadence, Marvell, and Micron.

3. ENVIRONMENTAL SUSTAINABILITY & CLIMATE COMMITMENTS
================================================================================
3.1 NVIDIA's Climate Strategy:
NVIDIA has committed to achieving 100% renewable electricity for its global offices and data
centers by the end of calendar year 2025. As of FY2024, approximately 76% of our global
electricity consumption was sourced from renewable energy certificates (RECs) and power
purchase agreements (PPAs).

3.2 Greenhouse Gas Emissions (Metric Tons CO2 Equivalent):
Emission Category                      FY2024          FY2023          FY2022
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scope 1 (Direct Emissions)            19,200          18,500          17,800
Scope 2 (Purchased Electricity,       54,300          42,100          38,600
         Market-Based)
Scope 2 (Purchased Electricity,       118,500          89,400          72,300
         Location-Based)
Scope 3 (Value Chain, estimated)      3,200,000       1,800,000       1,500,000

Note: The substantial increase in Scope 3 emissions reflects the dramatic growth in GPUs
sold and deployed in data centers globally. The majority of our Scope 3 footprint (estimated
85-90%) comes from the electricity consumed during the operational lifetime of NVIDIA GPUs
deployed in customer data centers (Category 11: Use of Sold Products).

3.3 Energy Efficiency of NVIDIA Products:
While our Scope 3 emissions have grown, we emphasize that NVIDIA accelerated computing
dramatically reduces the total energy required for AI workloads compared to CPU-only
alternatives:
- AI Training Energy Comparison: Training a GPT-3 175B model on NVIDIA A100 GPUs consumes
  approximately 1,287 MWh. Training the same model on optimized CPU clusters would require
  an estimated 12,870 MWh — approximately 10x more energy.
- AI Inference Energy Comparison: Serving 1 million inference queries on Llama 2 70B using
  NVIDIA H100 with TensorRT-LLM requires approximately 12 kWh. The same workload on
  optimized CPU infrastructure would require approximately 250 kWh — over 20x more energy.
- Data Center Accelerated Computing: If every CPU-based data center server performing AI and
  HPC workloads worldwide were replaced with NVIDIA accelerated computing, we estimate total
  global data center energy consumption for those workloads would decrease by 12x.

3.4 Water Stewardship:
Our Santa Clara headquarters campus uses recycled water for landscaping and cooling tower
makeup water, reducing potable water consumption by approximately 55 million gallons per year.
Total water withdrawal across all NVIDIA-operated facilities in FY2024 was approximately
420 million gallons.

3.5 E-Waste and Product Lifecycle:
NVIDIA GPU products are designed for a minimum operational lifetime of 5 to 7 years in data
center environments. We participate in electronic waste recycling programs and maintain
take-back programs for end-of-life products in compliance with the EU WEEE Directive and
similar regulations.

5. SUPPLY CHAIN RESPONSIBILITY & CONFLICT MINERALS
================================================================================
5.1 Responsible Sourcing:
NVIDIA is a member of the Responsible Business Alliance (RBA), formerly the Electronic
Industry Citizenship Coalition (EICC). We require our Tier 1 suppliers and key Tier 2
suppliers (including TSMC, ASE Group, Amkor Technology, SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron)
to comply with the RBA Code of Conduct, which covers:
- Labor: No forced labor, no child labor, maximum 60-hour work weeks, right to freedom of
  association, and legally mandated wages and benefits.
- Health & Safety: Occupational safety management, emergency preparedness, industrial hygiene.
- Environment: Hazardous substance management, wastewater treatment, air emission controls.
- Ethics: Anti-corruption, intellectual property protection, responsible sourcing of minerals.

5.2 Conflict Minerals (SEC Rule 13p-1):
NVIDIA files an annual Conflict Minerals Report (Form SD) with the SEC. Our products may
contain tin, tantalum, tungsten, and gold (3TG) originating from smelters and refiners in
the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) or adjoining countries. As of our most recent
filing:
- We identified 287 smelters and refiners in our supply chain for 3TG minerals.
- 98% of identified smelters (281 of 287) were certified as conformant by the Responsible
  Minerals Initiative (RMI) or equivalent third-party audit programs.
- 6 smelters were in the process of completing certification audits.
- We have not identified any smelters directly financing armed conflict in the DRC region.

6. DATA PRIVACY & AI ETHICS FRAMEWORK
================================================================================
6.1 NVIDIA AI Ethics Principles:
NVIDIA has published a set of AI Ethics Principles that guide our product development,
research, and customer engagements:
1. Safety: AI systems should be designed, developed, and deployed with safety as a primary
   consideration. We invest in research on AI alignment, robustness, and interpretability.
2. Trustworthiness: We strive for transparency about AI system capabilities and limitations.
3. Fairness: We work to identify and mitigate bias in AI models and datasets.
4. Privacy: We design our platforms and tools to enable privacy-preserving AI, including
   support for federated learning, differential privacy, and on-premises deployment.
5. Inclusivity: We work to ensure AI benefits are accessible to diverse communities globally.
6. Accountability: We maintain clear accountability for AI system behavior and outcomes.

6.2 AI Red Team:
NVIDIA maintains an internal AI Red Team that proactively tests our AI products and services
for safety vulnerabilities, adversarial robustness, bias, toxicity, and security issues before
public release. The AI Red Team works in collaboration with our product security incident
response team (PSIRT).

6.3 Data Privacy Compliance:
NVIDIA complies with applicable global data privacy regulations, including:
- EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)
- California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) / California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA)
- China Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL)
- Brazil General Data Protection Law (LGPD)
- India Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA)

NVIDIA does not sell GPU hardware or software in exchange for customer data. Our cloud
services (NGC, DGX Cloud, Omniverse Cloud) process customer data only as directed by the
customer and in accordance with our Data Processing Agreement (DPA).
