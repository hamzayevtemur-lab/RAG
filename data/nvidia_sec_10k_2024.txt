ITEM 1. BUSINESS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Overview:
NVIDIA pioneered accelerated computing to tackle challenges that ordinary computers cannot solve. Founded in 1993 by Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky, and Curtis Priem, the company initially revolutionized PC gaming through the invention of the Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) in 1999. Today, accelerated computing is recognized as the essential computing architecture for modern artificial intelligence (AI), high-performance computing (HPC), digital twins, robotics, and cloud gaming.

Accelerated computing delivers orders-of-magnitude performance gains and energy efficiency improvements over conventional general-purpose computing executed on central processing units (CPUs). Our platform integrates hardware—including GPUs, CPUs, Data Processing Units (DPUs), and interconnects—with specialized system software, CUDA (Compute Unified Device Architecture), application programming interfaces (APIs), software development kits (SDKs), libraries, and domain-specific applications.

Hardware Architectures & Platforms:
1. Hopper GPU Architecture:
   Introduced in fiscal year 2023 and ramped through fiscal year 2024, the Hopper architecture (featuring the NVIDIA H100 and H200 Tensor Core GPUs) is built on TSMC's 4N process node. It features Fourth-Generation Tensor Cores, a new Transformer Engine with FP8 precision, DPX instructions for dynamic programming, and second-generation asynchronous transfer engines. The H200 incorporates 141 gigabytes (GB) of high-bandwidth memory (HBM3e) running at 4.8 terabytes per second (TB/s), delivering double the memory capacity and 1.4x the bandwidth of the H100.

2. Grace CPU Architecture:
   The NVIDIA Grace CPU is our first proprietary data center CPU designed specifically for giant-scale AI and HPC workloads. Utilizing 72 ARM Neoverse V2 cores connected via our ultra-fast 3.2 TB/s bidirectional Scalable Coherency Fabric (SCF), Grace delivers up to 2x higher energy efficiency compared to traditional x86 server processors. In the Grace Hopper Superchip (GH200), Grace is coupled with a Hopper GPU across the NVLink-C2C interconnect, providing 900 GB/s of coherent bidirectional bandwidth.

3. Quantum & Spectrum Networking:
   NVIDIA networking solutions comprise InfiniBand and Ethernet technologies. Quantum-2 InfiniBand delivers 400 Gb/s per port with in-network computing (SHARPv3). Spectrum-4 Ethernet offers up to 51 Gb/s per port and provides zero-loss telemetry and adaptive routing for multi-tenant generative AI workloads in standard enterprise Ethernet data centers.

4. Software & Platform Ecosystem:
   The CUDA architecture, introduced in 2006, represents over 18 years of continuous optimization across millions of software developers. Today, more than 4.5 million developers globally build on CUDA. Our software stack includes:
   - NVIDIA AI Enterprise: A comprehensive commercial operating system and runtime for enterprise production AI.
   - TensorRT & TensorRT-LLM: Highly optimized inference compilers executing quantization (INT4, FP8, INT8), kernel auto-tuning, and KV-caching.
   - NeMo: An end-to-end cloud-native framework to build, customize, and run generative AI models with billions of parameters.
   - Omniverse: A platform for connecting and developing 3D pipelines, physics simulations, and industrial digital twins based on Universal Scene Description (OpenUSD).

Reportable Segments & Specialized Markets:
We have two reportable segments: Compute & Networking, and Graphics.
We target four specialized markets:
- Data Center: Accelerated infrastructure for training and deploying foundation models, large language models (LLMs), generative AI, cloud hyperscalers, sovereign AI clouds, and scientific discovery.
- Gaming: GeForce RTX GPUs, laptops, desktop systems, and GeForce NOW cloud gaming services.
- Professional Visualization (ProViz): Desktop and mobile workstations for 3D modeling, computer-aided design (CAD), visual effects, and medical imaging.
- Automotive: NVIDIA DRIVE platform covering DRIVE Orin and DRIVE Thor system-on-chips (SoCs) for autonomous vehicles, advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS), and AI cockpit solutions.

Manufacturing & Foundry Dependencies:
NVIDIA operates a fabless manufacturing model. We do not manufacture our own semiconductor wafers. We rely predominantly on Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited (TSMC) to fabricate our leading-edge silicon wafers on specialized semiconductor nodes (e.g., TSMC 4N). Advanced packaging technologies, including CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate), are performed primarily by TSMC and other outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) vendors including ASE Group and Amkor Technology. High Bandwidth Memory (HBM3 and HBM3e) stacks are supplied primarily by SK Hynix, Samsung Electronics, and Micron Technology.

ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Our business, financial condition, results of operations, and stock price are subject to numerous risks, including but not limited to:

1. Geopolitical Uncertainties and United States Export Regulations:
The U.S. government (USG) has imposed stringent export controls on our high-performance accelerated computing chips and networking equipment shipped to China and other designated countries (Country Groups D1, D4, and D5, including Russia, Iran, and certain Middle Eastern jurisdictions).
- In October 2022 and October 2023, the USG published interim final rules (the "Export Controls Rules") that lowered the performance thresholds for export licenses. These rules effectively restricted shipments of our A100, A800, H100, H800, L40, L40S, and RTX 4090 products to China, Macau, and other restricted destinations without an explicit USG export license.
- Historically, sales to customers headquartered in China (including Hong Kong) represented approximately 20% to 25% of our Data Center segment revenue. Following the implementation of the October 2023 export controls, Data Center revenue from China declined significantly to single-digit percentages in the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2024.
- If the USG expands export restrictions to lower performance tiers, bans additional software stacks, or revokes existing licenses, our competitive position, long-term market presence in China, and financial results will be materially and adversely affected.

2. Single-Source Manufacturing and Advanced Packaging Bottlenecks:
Because we rely on TSMC for the production of all our flagship GPU architectures and complex packaging (CoWoS), any operational disruption at TSMC—whether due to natural disasters, seismic activity in Taiwan, geopolitical escalation across the Taiwan Strait, regional power grid failures, or water shortages—would completely halt our ability to supply products. In fiscal year 2024, our revenue was constrained by the industry-wide availability of CoWoS packaging capacity and HBM3 memory supply rather than underlying consumer or enterprise demand.

3. Customer Concentration Risk:
A significant portion of our Data Center revenue is concentrated among a small number of cloud service providers (CSPs) and consumer internet companies. In fiscal year 2024, one indirect customer (Customer A) accounted for approximately 19% of total consolidated revenue through multiple system integrators, and another customer (Customer B) accounted for approximately 13% of total revenue. Any reduction in capital expenditure (CapEx) budgets by these hyperscalers could cause our revenue to decline precipitously.

4. Internal Silicon Development by Hyperscalers:
Major cloud customers, including Alphabet (Google TPU), Amazon Web Services (Trainium and Inferentia), Microsoft (Maia 100), and Meta (MTIA), are investing billions of dollars to design in-house custom Application-Specific Integrated Circuits (ASICs) for training and inference. To the extent our customers substitute custom internal silicon for NVIDIA GPU infrastructure, our long-term addressable market could be constrained.

The following discussion compares the financial performance of NVIDIA Corporation for Fiscal Year 2024 (ended January 28, 2024) with Fiscal Year 2023 (ended January 29, 2023) and Fiscal Year 2022 (ended January 30, 2022). All dollar amounts are expressed in millions, except per share data.

Executive Overview & Fiscal Year 2024 Highlights:
Fiscal year 2024 marked an inflection point in the computing industry. Generative AI and accelerated computing entered the mainstream enterprise adoption phase, driving explosive demand for NVIDIA accelerated platforms.
- Total Revenue: $60,922 million, an increase of 126% from $26,974 million in fiscal year 2023, and up 126% compared to $26,914 million in fiscal year 2022.
- Gross Margin: Expanded to 72.7% from 56.9% in fiscal year 2023, driven primarily by strong Data Center revenue growth, favorable product mix, and lower inventory reserve provisions.
- Operating Income: $32,972 million, an increase of 681% from $4,224 million in fiscal year 2023.
- Net Income: $29,760 million, representing diluted earnings per share (EPS) of $11.93, up 586% from $1.74 in fiscal year 2023.
- Cash Flows from Operating Activities: Generated $28,090 million in operating cash flow, compared to $5,641 million in fiscal year 2023.

Detailed Market Commentary:
1. Data Center:
Data Center revenue surged 217% to a record $47,525 million. Compute & Networking platforms drove the majority of this growth, led by shipments of the NVIDIA HGX Hopper architecture platform for training and inferencing large language models, generative AI applications, and recommender systems. Networking revenue grew 133% year-over-year to $13,016 million, powered by end-to-end Quantum-2 InfiniBand networking platforms. Revenue growth was led by cloud service providers, representing roughly half of our Data Center revenue, with consumer internet companies and enterprise customers making up the remainder.

2. Gaming:
Gaming revenue increased 15% to $10,447 million in fiscal year 2024, following a post-pandemic inventory correction that depressed fiscal year 2023 revenue. Growth was driven by the rollout of our GeForce RTX 40 Series desktop and laptop GPUs based on the Ada Lovelace architecture, featuring DLSS 3 neural rendering technology.

3. Professional Visualization:
Professional Visualization revenue was $1,553 million, up 1% compared to $1,544 million in fiscal year 2023. Strong enterprise demand for RTX Ada Lovelace architecture workstations in the second half of the year offset channel partner inventory digestion during the first half.

4. Automotive:
Automotive revenue reached $1,091 million, up 21% from $903 million in fiscal year 2023. Growth was driven by increased adoption of NVIDIA DRIVE Orin centralized compute platforms by global electric vehicle manufacturers and autonomous trucking developers.

Note 1: Significant Accounting Policies - Revenue Recognition
We recognize revenue under ASC 606 when control of promised goods or services is transferred to our customers, in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to be entitled to in exchange for those goods or services.
- Product Sales: Revenue from hardware products (e.g., HGX boards, PCIe GPUs, Superchips, InfiniBand switches, cables) is recognized at the point in time when shipment or delivery occurs, depending on contract terms.
- Software and Cloud Services: Software licenses that include term-based updates and technical support (e.g., NVIDIA AI Enterprise) are recognized ratably over the subscription term. Standalone perpetual licenses with significant standalone functionality are recognized at the point of delivery.

Note 2: Customer Concentration & Credit Risk
In fiscal year 2024, one indirect customer, Customer A, represented approximately 19% of total consolidated revenue through several primary system integrators. A second customer, Customer B, represented approximately 13% of total consolidated revenue. In fiscal year 2023, Customer A represented approximately 11% of consolidated revenue, and no other customer represented 10% or more.
As of January 28, 2024, two direct customers accounted for 14% and 11% of total accounts receivable, respectively.
